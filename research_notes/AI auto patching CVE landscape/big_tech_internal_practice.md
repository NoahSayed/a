# How Big Tech Internally Automates Vulnerability/Dependency Patching (as of Nov 2026)

Research note scope: internal practice at large companies (not products they sell), 2022 to Nov 2026. Method: web search plus a few page fetches. Many primary hosts were **blocked by this session's network policy**: engineering.atspotify.com, infoq.com, arxiv.org, shopify.engineering and groundcover.com could not be fetched. For those, claims rest on search-result snippets and secondary coverage, and are marked as such. The Microsoft SFI July 2026 report was fetched directly.

Overall picture (backed by the citations below): big companies have run *fleet-wide automated change* machinery for years (Google Rosie/LSCs, Spotify Fleet Management, Netflix Nebula campaigns). Since 2024 they have added LLM agents on top for migrations and bug fixing. Auto-merge **with no human** is documented only for low-risk routine maintenance PRs (Spotify, Netflix confidence-gated merges). For security fixes generated by LLMs, every big-company source I found keeps a human reviewer before landing (Google, Meta, DeepMind CodeMender).

## 1. Google: Rosie/LSCs, one-version monorepo, AI patching, CodeMender, Log4Shell

### Takeaway
Google's structure (a monorepo, the One Version Rule, Rosie for large-scale changes) makes "patch the dependency once, everywhere" possible. Since 2024 it has added Gemini-based fix pipelines internally: 15% of targeted sanitizer bugs fixed (2024), LLM migrations with about 50% estimated time saved (2025), and CodeMender running on internal Chrome/Android/Cloud/Ads/YouTube code (reported July 2026). All of these keep a human reviewer. I found no public data on Google's internal Log4Shell patch speed.

### Cited Findings
- **One Version Rule:** "There may only be one version of a package in //third_party", and the rule also applies to forks under different names. Upgrading a third-party library therefore means one upgrade for the whole monorepo. — [Google Open Source docs: The One Version Rule](https://opensource.google/docs/thirdparty/oneversion/)
- **Large-scale changes (LSCs) and Rosie:** an LSC is one logical change that has to be submitted as many separate changes, possibly touching thousands of files across hundreds of teams. Rosie splits a huge patch into reviewable shards (Software Engineering at Google, ch. 22, 2020). — [SWE at Google ch. 22](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch22.html); summary at [study notes](https://study-notes-dge.pages.dev/engineering-practices/software-engineering-at-google/chapters/ch22-large-scale-changes)
- Critique: under a strict single-version rule, every upgrade is a "big bang", and org-wide migrations can stall. — [j2kun, Monorepos and forced migrations](https://buttondown.com/j2kun/archive/monorepos-and-forced-migrations)
- **AI-powered patching (Jan 2024, Google Security Engineering tech report):** an automated pipeline prompts Gemini to fix sanitizer bugs found in unit tests. It "successfully fix[ed] 15% of sanitizer bugs" targeted, every fix had human review, and hundreds of bugs were patched. Pipeline stages: find, reproduce in isolation, LLM-generate fix, test, surface the best candidate to a human. — [Google Research pub](https://research.google/pubs/ai-powered-patching-the-future-of-automated-vulnerability-fixes/); [Google Security Blog Jan 2024](https://security.googleblog.com/2024/01/)
- Elie Bursztein (Google) said the technology is "far from" the goal of reliably and autonomously fixing 90 to 95% of bugs (2024). — [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchsecurity/feature/How-AI-driven-patching-could-transform-cybersecurity)
- **LLM code migrations at Google (FSE 2025, arXiv Apr 2025):** 39 migrations (int32 to int64 IDs) by 3 developers over 12 months. 595 changes and 93,574 edits were submitted; the LLM generated 74.45% of the changes and 69.46% of the edits. Developers *estimated* a 50% reduction in total migration time. Pipeline: Kythe finds references, a fine-tuned internal Gemini produces diffs, then build/test validation, then human review. — [arXiv 2504.09691](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09691); [FSE 2025 listing](https://conf.researchr.org/details/fse-2025/fse-2025-industry-papers/14/Migrating-Code-At-Scale-With-LLMs-At-Google)
- **CodeMender (DeepMind, announced Oct 6 2025):** a Gemini Deep Think-based agent that uses fuzzing, static analysis and differential testing. In its first 6 months it upstreamed 72 security fixes to **open-source** projects (codebases up to 4.5M LOC). Human researchers reviewed all patches before submission. — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/10/06/google-deepmind-unveils-Codemender-ai-agent-autonomously-patches-software-vulnerabilities/); [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/10/googles-new-ai-doesnt-just-find.html)
- CodeMender timeline in 2026: external developer access announced May 2026, Agent Platform integration at I/O 2026, managed-agent **preview July 21 2026** backed by the restricted "Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber" model. Google reportedly said Flash Cyber already runs through CodeMender across **internal Chrome, Android, Google Cloud, Ads and YouTube codebases**. This is secondary coverage of a vendor claim; I did not see a primary Google post. — [TechRepublic](https://techrepublic.com/article/news-google-flash-cyber-codemender/); [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4176170/google-folds-codemender-into-agent-ecosystem-amid-push-for-ai-led-appsec-2.html)
- A July 2026 headline reports Google AI "fixing 1,072" Chrome bugs. I saw only the headline and a snippet; the details are unverified. — [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/196408/ai/google-ai-supercharges-chrome-security-fixing-1072-bugs.html)
- **Log4Shell (Dec 2021):** Google's public output was ecosystem analysis. The Open Source Insights team found 35,863 Maven artifacts depending on vulnerable Log4j, and 4,620 were fixed within about a week. I found no source on how fast Google patched its *internal* monorepo. — [The Record](https://therecord.media/google-more-than-35000-java-packages-impacted-by-log4j-vulnerabilities/)

### Inferences
- With one version of log4j in //third_party, a Google-internal Log4Shell fix would in principle be a single third_party bump plus rebuild and redeploy. This is inferred from the One Version Rule; it is not documented.
- Google's AI patching is "LLM proposes, CI validates, human approves." I found no evidence of auto-landing LLM security patches.

### Gaps
- No public Google figures on internal time-to-patch for CVEs, on Rosie/LSC team size, or on per-fix LLM cost.
- I could not confirm from a primary Google source that CodeMender is used on internal code.

## 2. Meta: SapFix/Getafix, Engineering Agent, AutoPatchBench, TestGen

### Takeaway
Meta has run automated fix generation since 2018 (SapFix/Getafix, human-approved). In 2025 it reported an LLM agent (Llama-based, ReAct) that repairs test failures in production: 25.5% of generated fixes landed, all after human review. AutoPatchBench is a public benchmark; I found no evidence of how Meta uses it internally. I found no Meta post on how it patches third-party dependencies.

### Cited Findings
- **Getafix (Nov 2018):** learns fix patterns by hierarchical clustering of thousands of past human fixes. It suggests fixes for Infer static-analysis findings and powers SapFix. — [Engineering at Meta](https://engineering.fb.com/2018/11/06/developer-tools/getafix-how-facebook-tools-learn-to-fix-bugs-automatically/)
- **SapFix (Sep 2018):** tested patches go to a human reviewer, who accepts (the patch lands) or rejects (it is abandoned). "SapFix cannot deploy the fix to production on its own." It was used on 6 Android apps. — [Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/finding-and-fixing-software-bugs-automatically-with-sapfix-and-sapienz/); [SapFix paper](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10084761/1/SapFix-Automated-End-to-End-Repair-at-Scale-v2.pdf)
- **Agentic Program Repair from Test Failures at Scale (Meta, arXiv Jul 24 2025, Maddila et al.):** a ReAct agent on Llama, with a specialized 70B model competitive with vanilla Llama-405B offline. In a 3-month production trial, 80% of generated fixes were reviewed, 31.5% of those landed, which is **25.5% of all generated fixes**. Static analysis and tests act as a judge before human review. Reported ablation: test feedback raised the solve rate from 28.5% to 43.9%. (Taken from the abstract and summaries; arxiv.org was blocked, so I did not read the full PDF.) — [arXiv 2507.18755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18755v1); [papers.cool summary](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2507.18755)
  - One summary says these agents also fix SonarQube static-analysis issues. That may come from a related paper and is unconfirmed. — [chatpaper summary](https://chatpaper.com/paper/170551)
- **AutoPatchBench (Apr 29 2025, part of CyberSecEval 4):** 136 C/C++ fuzzing-crash samples across 11 crash types (the lite set is 113 per InfoQ, 120 per Meta docs; the sources conflict). Verification checks that the crash is fixed, that re-fuzzing finds no new crashes, and that program state matches the ground-truth fix. A baseline patch generator was released with it. It is presented as a public benchmark, with no stated internal deployment. — [Engineering at Meta](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/04/29/ai-research/autopatchbench-benchmark-ai-powered-security-fixes/); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2025/05/21/autopatchbench-meta-test-ai-bug-fixing-tools/)
- Caution: a DEV Community post titled "Retrospective: Automating dependency updates with Renovate 3.7.0 at Meta" (claiming a 0.2% regression rate after auto-merge) could not be verified and looks unofficial or possibly fabricated. **Do not cite it as Meta practice.** — [dev.to](https://dev.to/johalputt/retrospective-automating-dependency-updates-with-renovate-370-at-meta-1eaj)

### Inferences
- Meta's pattern has stayed the same from 2018 to 2025: automated generation, an automated test/static-analysis judge, and a mandatory human land decision.

### Gaps
- I found no Meta source on third-party dependency/CVE patching (Buck/monorepo third-party policy), on LLM TestGen in a security context, or on per-fix cost or team size.

## 3. Microsoft: SFI remediation automation, AI fixing

### Takeaway
Microsoft's SFI reports describe heavy automation for open-source and container patching (about 3M container vulns patched per month by automation, 550K+ critical/high OSS vulns remediated, per the July 2026 report) and AI agents for *finding* vulnerabilities (MDASH). The report I fetched does not say that Copilot Autofix or AI is used to *write* internal fixes.

### Cited Findings
- **SFI July 2026 progress report** (fetched directly): "More than 550,000 critical and high-risk open-source vulnerabilities were remediated." About **3 million container vulnerabilities patched per month through automation**. A multi-agent system built on **MDASH** (a multi-model agentic scanner) assesses source, identity config, network topology and runtime state; over 90% of its findings were confirmed by Microsoft security engineers. Engineering defaults block 83% of pipelines from reaching unapproved package endpoints. The report gives no time-to-patch metrics or SLAs and does not mention Copilot Autofix. — [Microsoft Security Blog, Jul 10 2026](https://microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/07/10/securing-our-future-july-2026-progress-report-on-microsofts-secure-future-initiative)
- The Nov 2025 SFI report stated an intent to "harness AI to increase engineering efficiency and enable... automated remediation" (from a snippet). — [Microsoft Security Blog, Nov 10 2025](https://microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2025/11/10/securing-our-future-november-2025-progress-report-on-microsofts-secure-future-initiative)
- April 2025 SFI report: 5 of 28 objectives near completion, 11 with significant progress. — [Microsoft, Apr 21 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2025/04/21/securing-our-future-april-2025-progress-report-on-microsofts-secure-future-initiative/)
- Copilot Autofix **product** figures (marketing; customer beta data May to Jul 2024): median time to fix a PR alert fell from 1.5 h to 28 min; XSS from 3 h to 22 min; SQLi from 3.7 h to 18 min. Security campaigns (GA Apr 8 2025) generate fixes for up to 1,000 alerts at a time. These are customer/product numbers, not Microsoft-internal ones. — [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/news-insights/product-news/secure-code-more-than-three-times-faster-with-copilot-autofix/); [AlternativeTo on campaigns](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/4/github-launches-security-campaigns-with-tools-like-copilot-autofix-to-reduce-security-debt)

### Inferences
- The "3M container vulns/month via automation" figure most likely reflects base-image rebuild and redeploy automation rather than LLM code edits. The report does not say which.

### Gaps
- I found no public statement on whether Microsoft or GitHub use Copilot Autofix on their own repos, or with what results.

## 4. Amazon/AWS: Log4Shell hot patch, Amazon Q Java upgrades

### Takeaway
Amazon's largest documented internal automation is the 2024 Amazon Q Java 8/11 to 17 upgrade campaign: tens of thousands of apps, a claimed 4,500 developer-years and $260M per year saved, all self-reported by the CEO. For Log4Shell, AWS built runtime hot-patching (Dec 2021), which itself shipped with container-escape bugs that were fixed in April 2022. That episode is a cautionary data point for automated emergency patching.

### Cited Findings
- **Andy Jassy (Aug 22 2024):** the average Java 17 upgrade went from about 50 developer-days to "a few hours". "Over 50% of production Java systems" were upgraded in under 6 months. The estimated saving is **4,500 developer-years**, plus about **$260M per year** from performance improvements. — [Simon Willison quoting Jassy](https://www.simonwillison.net/2024/Aug/24/andy-jassy-amazon-ceo); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/amazons-ai-assistant-saves-4500-years-of-development-time-ceo-andy-jassy-says/)
- AWS blog: "tens of thousands of production applications" were migrated from Java 8/11 to 17 with Amazon Q Developer. One report says 30,000 apps; another says over 1,000 developers were involved. — [AWS DevOps blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/devops/amazon-q-developer-just-reached-a-260-million-dollar-milestone)
- Note: these are self-reported estimates with no external audit. They concern a version-modernization campaign, not CVE response specifically.
- **Log4Shell hot patch (Dec 2021):** AWS released three hot-patching solutions (standalone, Kubernetes, ECS/Fargate) that detect running JVMs and patch them live. Unit 42 found that the patches allowed container escape and privilege escalation (any process binary named "java" was treated as a target). Fixed versions came out Apr 19 2022. — [Unit 42 via Tenable](https://www.tenable.com/blog/hot-patches-for-log4shell-introduced-multiple-vulnerabilities-in-amazon-web-services); [ITnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/when-aws-fixed-log4shell-it-created-new-vulnerabilities-579037)
- AWS said it had updated, or was updating, internal services that use Log4j, e.g. CloudFront (Dec 2021). — [RCR Wireless](https://www.rcrwireless.com/20211213/telco-cloud/hyperscalers-scramble-to-fix-apache-log4j-2-exploit)
- AWS re:Invent 2022 talk BOA204, "When security, safety, and urgency all matter: Handling Log4Shell", covers its internal response. I did not fetch the slides. — [AWS slides PDF](https://d1.awsstatic.com/events/Summits/reinvent2022/BOA204_When-security-safety-and-urgency-all-matter-Handling-Log4Shell.pdf)

### Gaps
- I found no published internal deployment timeline for Log4Shell at Amazon, and no per-upgrade LLM cost for the Q transformation campaign.

## 5. Other companies: Spotify, Netflix, Uber, Airbnb, Shopify, TRM Labs, others

### Takeaway
Spotify is the clearest documented case of fleet-scale maintenance PRs **auto-merged with no human**: more than 2.5M automated PRs, mostly auto-merged, plus the Claude-based Honk agent for complex migrations. Netflix runs confidence-scored auto-merge for fleet changes. Uber, Airbnb and Shopify report LLM agents doing migrations, remediation or high-volume changes, generally with review.

### Cited Findings
- **Spotify Fleet Management and Honk:**
  - Honk is a background coding agent inside Fleet Management, built on Claude Code via Anthropic's Agent SDK and run in Kubernetes. More than 1,500 AI-generated PRs had been merged by Nov 2025. — [Spotify Engineering, Nov 2025 (blocked; via search snippet)](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2025/11/spotifys-background-coding-agent-part-1); [ZenML LLMOps DB](https://zenml.io/llmops-database/ai-powered-background-coding-agents-for-large-scale-software-maintenance)
  - QCon London, Mar 2026: about 1,000 merged PRs every 10 days. — [InfoQ (blocked; via snippet)](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/03/spotify-honk-rewrite)
  - June 2026 post: more than **2.5 million automated maintenance PRs merged**, "the vast majority auto-merged with no human in the loop". This covers Fleet Management overall (dependency bumps and the like), not Honk alone. Fleetshift handles targeting, scheduling and tracking; Honk makes the edits. — [Spotify Engineering, Jun 2026 (blocked; via search snippet)](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/6/code-with-claude-coding-is-no-longer-the-constraint)
  - Oct 2026 InfoQ coverage of Spotify "AI quality controls" (verification layers for agent PRs). Blocked; I have no detail. — [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/10/spotify-ai-quality-controls)
  - Background (2023): Fleet Management series, "fleet-first mindset" and fleet-wide refactoring. — [Spotify Engineering 2023](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2023/4/spotifys-shift-to-a-fleet-first-mindset-part-1)
- **Netflix:**
  - Nebula (Gradle) with dependency locking and resolution rules; nightly automated PRs; a campaign tool for migrations *and security incidents*; Astrid tracks library versions in production. Scale is 3,000+ apps and 5,000+ repos (GOTO 2025 / DPE 2024 talks). — [GOTO session](https://gotopia.tech/sessions/3733/from-lag-to-lightning-confident-automated-changes-at-scale); [DPE 2024 slides](https://dpe.org/files/sessions/2024/Aubrey-and-Roberto-Netflix-Automated-Changes.pptx.pdf)

  - An aggregator says Netflix computes a single confidence score read at the merge gate: high confidence means auto-merge, followed by a post-merge check on main. Fleet-wide migrations reportedly went from 86 days to 26. These numbers are unverified secondary reporting. — [lavx aggregator](https://news.lavx.hu/article/netflix-s-confidence-chimp-how-one-team-cut-fleet-wide-migrations-from-86-days-to-26)
- **Uber:**
  - The CTO said on LinkedIn (2026) that the internal background coding agent writes about **1,800 code changes per week** "entirely", up from under 1% to 8% of all changes. Engineers review the PRs; a Slack notification arrives when a PR is ready. — [ShiftMag](https://shiftmag.dev/how-uber-engineers-use-ai-agents-8617/); [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/cto-pulse/news/uber-cto-says-95-of-engineers-now-rely-on-ai-internal-agents-push-1800-weekly-code-changes-13f26fbd-46a6-4317-a7e4-cd4026c5d531/)
  - Uber's stated aim is to have AI do "toil" such as upgrades and migrations. — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/how-uber-uses-ai-for-development)
  - **Cost signal:** Uber's AI coding spend reportedly "blew past" internal forecasts (April 2026 report). — [TechNewsday](https://technewsday.com/ubers-ai-coding-push-is-blowing-past-budgets-as-usage-surges)
  - Piranha (stale feature-flag cleanup, 2019 to 2023) predates this. I found no 2024 to 2026 Uber post on CVE or dependency-specific agents.
- **Airbnb (2025):** LLMs migrated about 3,500 Enzyme test files to React Testing Library in 6 weeks, against an estimated 1.5 years by hand. A brute-force retry loop worked best. Self-reported; this is a test-framework migration, not CVE patching. — [Airbnb Tech Blog](https://airbnb.tech/?p=366)
- **Shopify (2026):** the Infrastructure Security team applies the internal agent platform "River" to **autonomous vulnerability remediation**, starting from agent-discovered findings that carry recommended fixes, and the workflow verifies the PR's link to a vulnerability item. Shopify claims a "massive impact" but publishes no metrics I could see. The page was blocked; this comes from snippets. — [Shopify Engineering](https://shopify.engineering/river-vulnerability-remediation); [Digg summary](https://digg.com/ai/rm57pw5l)
- **TRM Labs (2026, mid-size):** an AI security orchestrator handles about 10K interactions per month, auto-closes 17% of alerts, and auto-approves low-risk infra PRs. For CVE fixes, "the agent creates PRs; humans merge them". — [ZenML summary](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/unified-ai-security-orchestrator-from-single-purpose-cve-agent-to-multi-workflow-autonomous-platform); [groundcover case study](https://www.groundcover.com/customer-stories/trm-part-2)

Morgan AI Research (Oct 2025, ASE AISM workshop):** a multi-agent Java dependency-upgrade framework (Summary, Control and Code agents) iterates until the build and tests pass, reporting 71.4% precision. It is research; production status is unclear. — [arXiv 2510.03480](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03480v2)
- **Elastic:** blog post on pairing Renovate with a Claude agent in CI to fix dependency-update breakages. — [Elastic Search Labs](https://www.elastic.co/search-labs/blog/ci-pipelines-claude-ai-agent)

### Inferences
- What is auto-merged at scale today is mostly *mechanical maintenance* (version bumps, config and lint changes) under strong CI. LLM-written code changes are still usually reviewed by a person, though Spotify's fleet numbers suggest some agent PRs may also flow through auto-merge paths. Whether Honk PRs specifically are auto-merged is unclear.

### Gaps
- I could not read Spotify's or Shopify's posts in full (hosts blocked), so I lack details on Spotify's auto-merge criteria and on Shopify River metrics.

## 6. Cost and team size; cheap DIY setups

### Takeaway
Big companies rarely publish team sizes or per-fix LLM cost. Here, "cost" means **platform teams** that own the fleet tooling, plus model spend; Uber reports that spend is exceeding forecasts. The best public per-patch cost benchmark is DARPA AIxCC (Aug 2025): about $152 average per competition task, roughly $32K to $103K total spend per finalist team. DIY for small teams is close to free: the Renovate app is free, the osv-scanner GitHub Action is free, and you pay only CI minutes.

### Cited Findings
- **DARPA AIxCC final (Aug 2025):** average cost per competition task (a proof of vulnerability, a patch, or a bundle) was about **$152**. Across all teams, total compute plus LLM spend was about $359K, with LLM spend about $82K over 1.9M queries. Synthetic vulns: 37% found, 25% patched (per a Trail of Bits presentation). Team totals: Team Atlanta $103.3K ($29.4K LLM), Trail of Bits $39.6K ($21.1K LLM), Theori $31.8K ($11.5K LLM). Theori had the lowest spend per vulnerability patched. Teams got $85K in Azure credits and $50K in LLM credits each. — [DARPA AIxCC results](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2025/aixcc-results); [Trail of Bits blog](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2025/08/09/trail-of-bits-buttercup-wins-2nd-place-in-aixcc-challenge/); [ToB main-stage slides](https://www.trailofbits.com/documents/AIxCC_Main_Stage_Presentation.pdf)
- Google's LLM migration study used **3 developers over 12 months** for 39 migrations. That is a rare published headcount. — [arXiv 2504.09691](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09691)
- Amazon: over 1,000 developers were involved in the Q Java upgrade campaign (AWS blog). — [AWS DevOps blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/devops/amazon-q-developer-just-reached-a-260-million-dollar-milestone)
- **DIY:** the Renovate GitHub App is free for public and private repos. Self-hosting via `renovatebot/github-action` is suitable for small teams and needs "no additional resources". Branch protection with required status checks is essential, because GitHub may otherwise auto-merge PRs with failing tests. Frequent cron runs (e.g. every 15 min) burn Actions minutes on private repos. — [Renovate GitHub App](https://github.com/apps/renovate); [Chainguard: running Renovate as a GitHub Action](https://www.chainguard.dev/unchained/running-renovate-as-a-github-action); [Medium: maintenance-free Renovate](https://medium.com/@superseb/maintenance-free-renovate-using-github-actions-workflows-d91d32ad854a)
- The osv-scanner GitHub Action (Google) is a reusable workflow. It annotates PRs and fails scheduled scans when vulns are found. — [OSV-Scanner GitHub Action docs](https://google.github.io/osv-scanner-v1/github-action/)
- **Vendor claim:** Mend Renovate Enterprise says it can auto-merge 60 to 80% of low-risk dependency PRs. This is marketing. — [Mend](https://www.mend.io/blog/mend-renovate-enterprise-cloud-dependency-updates-at-scale/)

### Inferences
- A small team's realistic stack is: Renovate (free) with auto-merge for patch/minor updates gated on required CI checks; osv-scanner or Dependabot alerts in CI; and optionally an LLM agent (Claude Code, Copilot or OpenHands) triggered only when an upgrade breaks the build. The marginal cost is CI minutes plus LLM tokens. AIxCC suggests autonomous find-and-fix runs on the order of tens to hundreds of dollars per vulnerability; a simple version bump with an LLM build-fix would be far cheaper. That last point is an inference, not measured.

### Gaps
- I found no published per-fix LLM cost from Google, Meta, Amazon, Spotify or Uber, and no published platform team sizes (e.g. Spotify Fleet Management, Netflix, Rosie).
- I did not verify current GitHub Actions free-minute allowances for private repos (the search did not return billing docs).

## 7. Is anyone running a fully autonomous loop (CVE, patch, test, auto-merge, deploy) with no humans? Guardrails?

### Takeaway
Partly. Fully autonomous merge and deploy is documented for **routine dependency/maintenance bumps** at Spotify (the "vast majority" of 2.5M PRs auto-merged) and Netflix (confidence-gated auto-merge), and probably underlies Microsoft's automated container patching. I found **no** documented big-company case of LLM-*authored* security fixes for new CVEs being merged and deployed with no human. Google, Meta, DeepMind and TRM Labs all keep a human merge gate for those.

### Cited Findings
- Spotify: more than 2.5M automated maintenance PRs, "vast majority auto-merged with no human in the loop" (Jun 2026). — [Spotify Engineering (via snippet)](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/6/code-with-claude-coding-is-no-longer-the-constraint)
- Netflix: a confidence metric at the merge gate (high confidence leads to auto-merge) plus a post-merge check on main. Secondary source. — [lavx](https://news.lavx.hu/article/netflix-s-confidence-chimp-how-one-team-cut-fleet-wide-migrations-from-86-days-to-26)
- Microsoft: about 3M container vulnerabilities patched per month "through automation" (Jul 2026). Human involvement is not specified. — [Microsoft SFI Jul 2026](https://microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/07/10/securing-our-future-july-2026-progress-report-on-microsofts-secure-future-initiative)

### Inferences
- In 2026 the state of practice is two-tier: (1) deterministic version bumps are auto-merged and auto-deployed under CI, canary and confidence gates; (2) LLM-written code fixes get automated validation followed by human review. The boundary is moving: Uber's 1,800 agent changes per week and Spotify's Honk throughput show review is becoming the bottleneck. I found no public evidence that big companies have removed the human gate for LLM security patches.

### Gaps
- I found no public end-to-end time-to-remediate figure (CVE disclosure to deployed in production) for any big company in 2024 to 2026.
