# Verification of tech/academic claims (checked 2026-11-19)

## Summary

Method limit: WebFetch could not resolve arxiv.org or darpa.mil (network allowlist; both need allowing for a deeper check). Verification therefore used WebSearch result extracts, which often quote the primary page (arXiv abstracts, NVD, OSV Swagger) and sometimes only secondary coverage. "Confirmed" below means the primary page text was quoted in search results or two or more independent sources agree. No full primary page was read. Status of each claim is marked.

Result: of 20 claims, 13 confirmed, 5 partly confirmed (with a correction), 0 contradicted, 2 unverifiable. Nothing looks fabricated. The notes are broadly reliable, but these points change the picture for a build decision:

1. **PatchEval 23% is stale.** The repo shipped "PatchEval-Verified" on 2026-07-24 with a changed harness. Its leaderboard lists 83.9% (193/230) for a codex/GPT-5.6 setup. The harness changed, so the numbers are not comparable. Do not cite 23% as the current state of the art.
2. **Vul4J 24.8% is a single-model, zero-shot result** (Gemini 2.0 Flash per the abstract; the HTML says "Gemini 3.0 Flash"). It is not a general ceiling for LLM repair.
3. **Fortune 500 attribution is wrong in the notes.** "At least five Fortune 500 companies (and Google)" is Aikido's PromptPwnd finding, not a Clinejection finding. Clinejection is a single-target incident at Cline.
4. **The "170 silent failures" study is identified**: arXiv 2609.10548. The notes' "seven frameworks" could not be confirmed. A companion paper (2610.06163) says six frameworks.
5. **SoK "91/99 accuracy" and "Patch-SARIF 1/3"** could not be confirmed from search extracts. Competition-level team accuracies were confirmed (Atlantis 91.27%).
6. **The NVD April 2026 change is solid and official** (NIST page, released 2026-04-15, updated 2026-04-17).
7. **The GTIG report is real but thin.** Google gave no victim count, no success rate and no measured patch-to-exploit time, and the agents hallucinated. The notes' framing of "shrinking grace period" is the press's inference, not a Google measurement.

## Additional items checked

- **"170 silent failures"** (from architecture notes): source identified as arXiv 2609.10548, "When Passing Tests Hides Vulnerabilities: An Empirical Study of Silent Failures in Agentic Systems" (170 cases; 78 of them, 45.9%, in "Insecure API Preserved" and "Weak Mitigation"). The framework count is unconfirmed (a companion paper, 2610.06163, uses six). The notes attributed it to PatchBench/EviRepair, which is wrong. [arXiv 2609.10548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10548).
- **PatchBench follow-up:** "On the Reliability of LLM-Based Vulnerability Patching Benchmarks" (arXiv 2610.10150) exists; useful to read for further caveats on benchmark validity. Not read.
